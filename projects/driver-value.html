Driver Value Segmentation — a 3-level Bayesian model.

The supply-side counterpart to the passenger CLV model. For every independent driver in Algeria, a Bayesian MBG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma system (pymc-marketing) answers three separate questions — is this driver still active, how often will they work, and what is an active day worth — over a 60-day horizon. The three levels are cut into data-driven bands and combined into a retention priority matrix that tells Supply Ops and CRM exactly which drivers to protect, rescue, or win back.

The problem

On the demand side, a passenger's value is fairly clean to measure: rides × spend. On the supply side it isn't. A driver only completes the trips the matching engine hands them, so modelling churn on completed trips conflates "they stopped showing up" with "they stopped getting matched" — a funnel-ownership confound that would have the model blaming drivers for marketplace conditions.

Supply Ops also needed more than one number. A single "driver LTV" score hides the decision that actually matters: a high-value driver drifting away needs a very different intervention from a low-value driver who is perfectly stable. The goal was a model whose levels could be read independently — and then combined into an operational priority list.

Three levels, one composite

Each level is modelled, validated, and banded separately before being combined.

Design decisions, made explicitly

1 · The transaction is an active day — not a trip, not a shift

BG/NBD-family models assume a customer transacts at some rate λ until they silently churn. Picking the driver-side analogue of a "transaction" decides what the model actually measures:

  • Completed trips are system-mediated — they measure matching, not driver intent.
  • Raw shift starts are driver-owned but contained a confirmed toggle artifact (drivers logging 300+ shifts in 90 days), which would corrupt a Poisson count.
  • Active calendar days are driver-owned and immune to the toggle artifact. Collapsing to the day level also removes intra-day burstiness and keeps the Poisson assumption honest.

2 · Value is capacity, not a churn-weighted expectation

Expected active days already integrate P(alive). Multiplying them by P(alive) again to get "value" would double-count churn and make the churn and value axes of the priority matrix collapse onto each other. Instead, value is a capacity measure — the driver's work rhythm × EUR earnings per active day, if they stay. The result: corr(capacity value, P(alive)) = +0.08, so the two axes are genuinely independent and the matrix has no empty cells.

3 · A 2-month test window to stabilise labels

A part-time driver who works twice a month produces an extremely noisy "active / not active" label in a 1-month window. The holdout window — and the prediction horizon — are locked at 60 days, with an assertion in the pipeline that keeps both in lockstep. Holdout metrics are then stratified by frequency band so performance on the casual tail stays visible instead of being averaged away.

4 · Leakage-proof temporal design

Training covers January–June 2026, the holdout July–August. Evaluation features are frozen at the start of the test window, and each driver's market is attributed once from a trailing 12-month window and reused for every dataset — so a driver can never be counted in one country during training and another during evaluation.

Modelling & validation

Hard gates before MCMC

A validation stage enforces the mathematical preconditions of pymc-marketing models — no negative frequencies, recency ≤ T, no nulls, strictly positive monetary value — and fails loudly before any expensive sampling starts.

Bayesian fit with convergence gates

The Modified BG/NBD was chosen over classic BG/NBD because single-active-day drivers are a large cohort, and the classic model cannot let them churn immediately — it would badly overstate their retention. Both MBG/NBD and Gamma-Gamma are fit by MCMC and gated on R-hat < 1.01, ESS > 400, and zero divergences. The Gamma-Gamma frequency/value independence assumption is checked via correlation before the fit is trusted.

Data-driven segment boundaries

No hand-picked thresholds anywhere — each level's bands are learned from data:

  • Churn bands — a 3-leaf 1D decision tree on calibrated P(alive) against observed holdout churn found cuts at 0.48 and 0.75 (likely churned · at risk · active).
  • Cadence bands — 1D k-means on expected active days, converted into "days between active days": daily (≤ 2.4), frequent (≤ 4.9), regular (≤ 14.9), occasional, plus effectively inactive.
  • Value tiers — knee-point detection on the Lorenz curve: the top 28.4% of drivers hold 78.6% of capacity value (critical), the top 57.2% hold 95.4% (important).

The most actionable finding was the reactivation pool: drivers dormant for ~3.5 months who, when they did work, worked nearly every third day. They hold the majority of latent capacity value — a supply lever that pure churn scoring would have written off as "lost".

Holdout results — and honest caveats

  • Level 1 — churn. Holdout AUROC 0.78, Brier skill score 0.27 over a 46.9% base rate. Excellent on core drivers (0.84 AUROC at 16+ active days), weaker on the casual tail (0.65 for single-day drivers) — those predictions are flagged confidence = low so they don't drive premium retention spend alone.
  • Level 2 — habit. Aggregate active days within +5.0% of actual (184K predicted vs 175K observed), MAE 3.35 days per driver — reliable for city-level supply planning. Slight under-prediction of full dormancy, and a small "resurrection" uptick in the bottom deciles that argues for a dedicated win-back trigger.
  • Level 3 — value. Aggregate holdout value error −7.0% (€1.16M predicted vs €1.25M actual), MAE €2.11 per active day. Drivers outside the Gamma-Gamma domain (no repeat days) get the population mean and are flagged value_is_imputed.

Production

The pipeline is parameterised by market (Algeria and France configs), runs on GCP, and stores fitted models, the calibrator, and run metadata as versioned artifacts in Cloud Storage. Scores are written to BigQuery idempotently — re-running a reference date replaces that month's rows instead of duplicating them — while full history is kept so later cycles can check whether predicted segments actually behaved as forecast. Each row carries model version, holdout AUROC, and a confidence flag for downstream CRM guards.
