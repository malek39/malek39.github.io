Bayesian Passenger CLV Model.

Production Bayesian customer lifetime value model for 1.8 million Heetch passengers, built on MBG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma using pymc-marketing. Trained on 24 months of historical ride and payment data; predicts each passenger's expected revenue over the next 3 months with full posterior uncertainty. Scoring runs weekly on GCP Vertex AI; the model retrains every 4 weeks. Outputs flow directly into Braze for CRM activation.

The problem

The Growth team was allocating acquisition spend across channels (Adjust, Meta, Google, Apple Search) using short-window CAC and payback metrics. This systematically underweighted channels that brought in users with longer payback periods but higher lifetime value — exactly the kind of users you most want to acquire. CRM, meanwhile, was targeting based on static RFM, with no probabilistic view of future value.

Traditional LTV approaches fall in two traps. Either they're too simple — ARPU × retention curve — and miss the heterogeneity in how passengers actually behave. Or they're too complex — deep learning on event sequences — and lose interpretability, which matters when the VP needs to understand why a channel or segment is being recommended.

I chose the Bayesian probabilistic approach deliberately: it gives accurate per-passenger predictions with calibrated uncertainty, while keeping each component interpretable. The output isn't just a number — it's a posterior distribution, which means Growth can make decisions with full visibility into how confident the model is on any given cohort.

Approach

I used MBG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma, implemented in pymc-marketing — a probabilistic framework that decomposes CLV into a frequency component and a monetary component, each fit by Bayesian inference rather than maximum likelihood.

Frequency component — MBG/NBD

The Modified Beta-Geometric / Negative Binomial Distribution model predicts the expected number of future rides per passenger, conditional on their full history. Two latent processes:

  • A Poisson process governing how frequently an active passenger takes rides.
  • A Beta-distributed dropout probability — the chance that a passenger goes inactive after any given ride.

The "modified" variant (MBG/NBD vs the classical BG/NBD) handles passengers with zero repeat purchases properly — a meaningful population in ride-hailing that the classical model treats incorrectly.

Monetary component — Gamma-Gamma

Conditional on the frequency model, the Gamma-Gamma model predicts the average revenue per ride for each passenger. It assumes monetary value per transaction follows a Gamma distribution and that spending pattern is independent of frequency pattern — an assumption I validated empirically on holdout data before deploying.

Bayesian inference via MCMC

Both models are fit using Markov Chain Monte Carlo in pymc-marketing, which gives a full posterior over the model parameters rather than point estimates. This is the key advantage: every CLV prediction comes with an uncertainty band, which propagates into downstream segmentation and budgeting decisions.

# MBG/NBD: fit frequency model with MCMC mbg = MBGModel(data=df_passenger_summary) mbg.fit( draws=2000, tune=1000, chains=4, # 4 parallel chains for convergence diagnostics target_accept=0.95 ) # Posterior predictive: expected rides over next 3 months (T = 90 days) expected_rides_3m = mbg.expected_purchases( customer_id=df.customer_id, future_t=90, return_mean=False # return full posterior samples ) # Gamma-Gamma: fit monetary model, combine for CLV gg = GammaGammaModel(data=df_passenger_summary) gg.fit(draws=2000, tune=1000, chains=4) clv_3m_posterior = expected_rides_3m * gg.expected_customer_spend(...)

Convergence diagnostics

Ran 4 parallel MCMC chains and validated convergence via standard Bayesian diagnostics — R-hat < 1.01, effective sample size > 1000 per parameter, no divergent transitions. These checks run automatically on every retrain and block deployment if they fail. This is the difference between "we fit a Bayesian model" and "we deploy a Bayesian model that's actually trustworthy."

Probability calibration

For the binary "will purchase in next 3 months" signal derived from the frequency model, I applied isotonic calibration on a holdout fold to ensure predicted probabilities match observed empirical rates. Calibration matters when downstream consumers (CRM, budget allocation) make threshold decisions on the score.

Training data & features

Trained on 24 months of historical passenger data — full ride history, payment records, lifecycle transitions, and behavioural signals. The 24-month window was chosen to capture full seasonality cycles while avoiding stale behaviour from passengers who behave very differently today than two years ago.

Beyond the core RFM signals (recency, frequency, monetary value), I engineered features that capture meaningful passenger behaviour patterns:

  • Payment method mix — card vs cash distribution, a strong signal in markets where one payment type dominates.
  • Ride radius — median and variance of pickup/destination geographies, capturing commuter-heavy vs social-heavy patterns.
  • Time-of-day distribution — morning / evening / weekend skew.
  • Lifecycle stage from the behavioural segmentation pipeline.
  • Cancellation behaviour — cancel rate and recency, a leading indicator for disengagement that pure frequency models miss.

Production pipeline

Weekly scoring · monthly retrain

The pipeline runs on a two-tier cadence:

  • Weekly scoring. Every week, the latest model scores all 1.8M passengers and materialises CLV + uncertainty + decile + lifecycle stage to a BigQuery table.
  • Monthly retrain. Every 4 weeks, the full pipeline retrains on the most recent 24 months. Convergence diagnostics gate deployment — if R-hat or ESS thresholds fail, the previous model continues scoring until the next retrain succeeds.

Decoupling scoring (cheap, weekly) from retraining (expensive, monthly) was deliberate. It gives CRM fresh scores every week without burning compute on unnecessary refits, and keeps model parameters stable enough that downstream segment definitions don't drift week-to-week.

Orchestration on GCP Vertex AI

  • Training job runs on Vertex AI Workbench with managed pymc / JAX backend for MCMC acceleration.
  • Scoring job materialises predictions to BigQuery, partitioned by scoring date.
  • Looker dashboard reads from the materialised table — channel × cohort × CLV breakdown with confidence intervals, used by the VP of Growth for budget reviews.
  • Braze sync runs after each scoring run, pushing fresh CLV deciles and lifecycle stages to CRM-ready audience segments for same-day activation.
Top decile captured 57% of total holdout revenue — meaning CRM and retention spend can be concentrated on the 10% of passengers who actually drive over half the business.

Outcome

Replaced single-window CAC analysis with a forward-looking, uncertainty-aware CLV view. Surfaced acquisition channels with high CAC but excellent CLV that had been underfunded under the old framework, and identified passenger segments where retention spend was being wasted on users with structurally low future value.

The model is now part of the standard Growth toolkit — feeding the OKR framework, CRM targeting, and acquisition budget allocation decisions.
